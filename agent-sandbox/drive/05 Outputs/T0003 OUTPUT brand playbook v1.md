## 1. Positioning

**One line:**
> A lawyer who writes the rules for new frontiers: AI inside democratic institutions, and law for the Moon.

**Three sentences:**
> Kwame Newton is a Harvard-trained lawyer who works where emerging technology meets democratic governance. He drafted the U.S. House's first AI use policy, advised Ghana's Parliament on modernization, and has written and spoken on space law since 2020, from the Artemis Accords to how export controls behave beyond Earth. His through-line is a civil-rights instinct: new frontiers, on Earth or off it, should be governed in the open, by rules that everyone can read.

**Why this framing:** it ties three threads together without making him pick one: AI governance (the job-search lane), space law (the passion and publication record), and democracy and civil rights (Brennan Center, Public Citizen, ACS, House Democracy Partnership). "Rules" is the common noun. Each thread is backed by a credential a reader can verify.

Kwame sends every pitch. Check each venue's current CFP or submission rules first; I didn't verify deadlines.

### A. SpaceNews: op-ed
> **Pitch: "The Outer Space Treaty turns 60. Its hardest test is a customs form."**
> On January 27, 2027, the Outer Space Treaty turns sixty. The debates are about lunar resources and the Artemis Accords, but the rules most shaping multinational lunar missions are U.S. export controls written for a world of borders. I'm a Harvard-trained lawyer, a former National Space Society and For All Moonkind legal fellow, and the author of NSS's input to the UN working group on space resources. In 900 words I'd argue that ITAR and EAR now act as unwritten space law, and propose three fixes that keep security intact. Timed for the anniversary week.

### B. International Space Development Conference (ISDC 2027, National Space Society): talk
> **Talk: "Sixty Years of Nobody's Moon: What the Outer Space Treaty Can Still Do"** (20 minutes)
> A plain-English tour of the 1967 treaty's five load-bearing articles, the ones the Artemis era is stress-testing right now. Drawing on the speaker's interactive OST history site and his work as an NSS legal fellow, the talk shows where the treaty is holding, where it's bending, and what a 2030s amendment by practice might look like. Kwame Newton spoke at ISDC before and judged the 2025 Manfred Lachs moot court.

### C. Lawfare or Tech Policy Press: essay
> **Pitch: "Before Congress regulates AI, it had to regulate itself"**
> In 2024 the U.S. House adopted HITPOL 8.0, its first institutional AI use policy. I helped draft it. The process is a template other legislatures are now asking about, including Ghana's Parliament, which I advised in August 2026. The essay covers what an internal AI policy for a legislature has to settle (use-case inventories, NIST alignment, bipartisan ownership) and why a legislature's own AI governance is a precondition for credible lawmaking on AI. About 1,500 words, no internal or non-public material.
